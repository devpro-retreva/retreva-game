Badagry (`destinations/badagry.ts`) depicts real public monuments by name — the First Storey Building (1845), the Badagry Heritage Museum, the Agia Tree monument, the jetty to Gberefu Island and, across the lagoon, a stylised Point of No Return arch — all modelled procedurally, not copied from photos; its businesses and people are fictional. The slave-trade history is stated plainly and is never the object of a job: the paid work there is guiding a visitor and carrying fish.

Abuja (`destinations/abuja.ts`) names Wuse Market and Aso Rock (a stylised granite dome on the horizon, modelled procedurally); its businesses and people are fictional, and the Nnamdi Azikiwe airport terminal uses the same generic template as the other airports.

Calabar (`destinations/calabar.ts`) names real places on the Marina — the Slave History Museum, the Old Residency (National Museum) and the river front — and the carnival tradition, with short factual blurbs; Mma Affiong, Ekaette, Nsa, Tolani, Okokon and their businesses are fictional, and the Margaret Ekpo airport terminal uses the generic airport template. Everything is modelled procedurally.

Ibadan (`destinations/ibadan.ts`) names real places in and around Dugbe — Cocoa House, Dugbe Market, Mapo Hall and Bower's Tower on Oke-Are — with short factual blurbs, all modelled procedurally (a banded tower, a columned hall, a hill with a white tower, a field of brown roofs). Mama Risi, Iya Sikiru, Alhaji Lateef, Dayo, Baba Tunde and their businesses are fictional. The railway stations (`station.ts`) use the real station names (Mobolaji Johnson Station, Ebute Metta; Obafemi Awolowo Station, Moniya) with generic signage; the train operator "Western Express Rail" and its livery are fictional, and fares follow the railway's published adult fares.

Abeokuta (`destinations/abeokuta.ts`) names real places in Itoku and Ake — Olumo Rock, the Itoku adire market and Centenary Hall — with short factual blurbs; Olumo is the procedural `monolith` granite, the adire cloths a procedural `clothline` prop. Iya Alaro, Baba Ogunde, Kemi, Mama Ofada, Adaeze, Taiwo and their businesses are fictional, as are the bus conductors Alhaji Sule (Ikeja/Ojota) and Rasaki (Dugbe). Adire pattern names (oniko, eleko, alabere) are the traditional technique names.

Osogbo (`destinations/osogbo.ts`) names real places — the Ataoja's palace, Oja Oba market, the town's art tradition and the Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove (UNESCO World Heritage Site, 2005) — with short factual blurbs. The grove is shown only from its gate, with procedural sculptures written as being "in the spirit of" the New Sacred Art (not copies of any real work); no job goes into it. The festival event is a fictional evening show; its text says the real festival procession is free and held once a year in August. Mama Folake, Iya Iyan, Yinka, Kwame, Bayo and their businesses are fictional.

Benin City (`destinations/benin.ts`) names real places around King’s Square — the National Museum, Igun Street and its guild of bronze casters, the Emotan statue at Oba Market and the old city moat — with short factual blurbs; the statues are procedural (`statue` prop), not copies of real works, and the bronzes on sale are described as new pieces cast in the old style. Pa Osayande, Mama Osas, Eghosa, Mama Ivie, Nosa, Uyi and their businesses are fictional; the Benin Airport terminal uses the generic airport template.

Enugu (`destinations/enugu.ts`) names real places in Ogui — Ogbete Main Market, Michael Okpara Square, the Coal Camp — and Milliken Hill with the Ngwo pine forest, with short factual blurbs; the escarpment and pines are procedural. Nne Ada, Mama Chinyere, Uche, Fatima, Chidi and their businesses are fictional; the Akanu Ibiam terminal uses the generic airport template.

No third-party 3D models, textures, photos, music or sound files are shipped. City packs (`dist/packs.json`: Accra, Dubai) contain only the project's own data/code. Brands, logos and real businesses are not depicted; place names (Yaba, Osu, Oxford Street…) are real, businesses on them are fictional.

## If a GLB character or prop is added later

Budget for a vehicle prop (proposed for the danfo test): ≤ 3,000 triangles, one mesh, one material, one ≤ 1024² base-colour JPEG, ≤ 300 KB GLB, no Draco/meshopt (no decoder download). Run it through `tools/blender/optimize_glb.py`, which checks all of these, and look at a `render_preview.py` image before integrating. Keep the Meshy master out of `public/`.

The renderer (three.js via @react-three/fiber) can load GLB/glTF. Before integrating one: keep the procedural version as the fallback, record the source/creator/date/licence here, target ≤ 15k triangles and one 1024² texture for a character on the Low tier, test on Low/Medium/High in the `responsive`, `device` and `city` playtests, and measure draw calls/triangles with `runtime.perf`.
